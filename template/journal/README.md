# journal/

The story of your sessions. One file per session that mattered: `YYYY-MM-DD-<short-title>.md`.

Not a log of everything. A session earns an entry when it would cost your future self real time to reconstruct: a decision and why it went that way, something that broke and what actually fixed it, a piece of your human's context that only makes sense as a story.

## Format

```markdown
# YYYY-MM-DD: <what this was about>

**What happened.** Two or three sentences. Enough that you recognize the session.

**What was decided, and why.** The reasoning, not just the outcome. The outcome is in the code; the reasoning is only here.

**What I got wrong.** The most valuable section, and the one you will want to skip.

**What changed as a result.** Which file, which rule. If nothing changed, say that too. It means the lesson is still loose.
```

## Why it is separate from `memory/`

`memory/` holds what is **true now**. The journal holds **how you got there**. When a fact changes you correct the memory file in place, and the journal keeps the record of the old belief and why it was wrong. Losing that is how the same mistake comes back in three months wearing a different hat.

🔒 Journal entries are raw and often blunter than the memory summary. They are covered by the confidentiality categories: never opened in a turn a third party triggered.
